Getting started

Not a certified or flight-qualified instrument. Do not use as a primary or emergency attitude reference, or to fly without external visual reference. Read the operational limitations before use.

Android 8.0 or later, a working gyroscope and an accelerometer are required. Magnetic heading needs a magnetometer; barometric altitude and flight level need a pressure sensor. GPS indications need device location and precise-location permission. Android compatibility alone does not guarantee suitable sensors.

  1. Install only an owner-authorized build. See download availability and installation.
  2. Secure the phone in its intended mounting on the ground, then open GlideHorizon and allow the estimator to settle.
  3. Open the menu near the upper-left of the instrument. Choose Display orientation, then complete Ground calibration.
  4. Check bank and pitch against known, safely controlled movements before configuring optional displays or the XCSoar feed.

Perform installation, calibration and configuration on the ground. The initial assumption of an upright phone with its screen toward the pilot is not a valid calibration for an arbitrary installation.

Mounting and calibration

Select portrait, landscape or an inverted orientation to match the screen. A quarter-turn within the screen plane can reuse the mounting calibration; changing the mount's inclination or which way the phone faces requires a new calibration.

Stationary calibration

  1. Stop any recording in Diagnostics. Open Ground calibration and select Stationary.
  2. Select Phone back only when the phone's back points forward and its screen faces the pilot. Use Saved axis only for a previously verified direction.
  3. Enter Known ground pitch (deg, nose-up +) and a useful mount description. Zero is correct only for a reference that really is level in pitch.
  4. Confirm the installation is stationary, wings level at the entered pitch, and facing the selected forward direction.
  5. Select Capture reference. Keep it completely still through the settling delay and three-second capture.
  6. Review the result. Choose Accept and save only if the reference and mounting assumptions were correct, then Done. Allow settling and check the response again.

Wing-tilt calibration

Use Wing tilts when the roll-axis direction needs to be measured. Capture the known stationary reference, then follow the prompts for a right tilt and a left tilt with unchanged pitch, holding still for each capture. Return to the original pose and select Validate level. Review the movements and return check before accepting. Only perform movements that are safe for the aircraft or test fixture.

Gyro movement between still captures is recorded, including lifting, twisting and repositioning supports. Keep movement about one consistent fore-aft axis: at least 95% of measured rotation energy must align with it. A cross-axis failure reports the measured fit and right/left moving-sample counts. Passing the still captures does not guarantee the intervening movements will pass.

For a flat, screen-up phone with its top edge pointing forward, a secure lengthwise pivot can help make right/left rocking repeatable. Keep it in place and support the phone level for both reference captures. Moving a loose support between sides can introduce extra axes. Use a stable fixture, avoid accidental screen touches and cable pull, and perform this setup only on the ground.

Cancelled or failed attempts retain the previous saved calibration. Stationary calibration assumes the forward direction; it does not measure it.

Opening calibration turns off the XCSoar feed. Re-enable it explicitly after checking the result. Calibration does not qualify the app for flight use.

Connect to XCSoar

The feed sends bank and pitch only using the GDL90 ForeFlight AHRS extension. It does not send heading, airspeed, GPS position, altitude or flight level. One destination is active at a time.

Accept a ground calibration first. Turn transmission off before changing modes or destinations. In XCSoar, edit an unused device slot under Config > Devices (sometimes Instrument), leaving other sensor connections unchanged.

Local: both apps on one phone

  1. In XCSoar, select UDP listener or UDP-port, driver GDL90, and a port such as 4000.
  2. In GlideHorizon, open XCSoar feed, choose Local and enter the same port.
  3. Enable Transmit live attitude. Check XCSoar's device status and ForeFlight AHRS reception.

Local mode uses 127.0.0.1 and needs no Wi-Fi connection. Some XCSoar versions label the numeric field TCP-port even when UDP is selected.

Wi-Fi: another device

  1. Connect both devices to the same trusted network, or the receiver to the sensor phone's hotspot.
  2. Configure XCSoar on the receiving device as a UDP listener with the GDL90 driver.
  3. Find the receiver's IPv4 address. In GlideHorizon, choose Wi-Fi, enter that address and the matching port, then enable transmission.
  4. Check reception and bank/pitch response in XCSoar.

Use a private IPv4 address in 10.x.x.x, 172.16.x.x through 172.31.x.x, or 192.168.x.x, ending in 1 to 254. Hostnames, IPv6, broadcast and multicast destinations are not supported. Guest-network isolation, VPNs, firewalls and changing addresses can block delivery. LAN UDP is not encrypted or authenticated.

Bluetooth: another Android device

  1. Enable Bluetooth on both devices. Choose Bluetooth in GlideHorizon and grant Nearby devices permission when requested.
  2. Select Pair receiver. From the receiver's Android Bluetooth settings, pair with the sensor phone and confirm the matching prompts. Contacts and call-history sharing are unnecessary.
  3. In GlideHorizon, choose Refresh paired, select the device running XCSoar, and enable transmission.
  4. In XCSoar, select the paired sensor phone as the Bluetooth port and choose GDL90. Save, check reception and reconnect if necessary.

This uses Bluetooth Classic SPP, not BLE. GlideHorizon is the server; XCSoar connects as the client. Do not select Bluetooth-server in XCSoar. No UDP port or baud rate is needed.

SENDING is not confirmation of reception. Check XCSoar itself. Only fresh usable calibrated attitude is sent, but degraded estimates may still be transmitted. XCSoar may retain old values after packets stop; do not assume an automatic fallback.

Pitch trim, correction gain and experimental gyro low-pass filtering affect the transmitted estimate. The destination and enabled setting are saved. Quit stops runtime transmission but retains that preference; explicitly disabling the feed, selecting Stop XCSoar feed or opening calibration saves it as off.

Display controls

Horizon, heading and turn

The fixed aircraft symbol is the reference for bank and pitch. The heading bar shows MAG for a magnetic reference, MAG / HOLD when propagating an existing reference without current magnetic correction, or REL for relative turn. Relative zero is not north; magnetic heading is neither true heading nor GPS track.

In Display elements, Turn indicator adds the overlay and Turn and slip page independently enables the dedicated page. Both default off. Close menus, then swipe left from the horizon to open the page and right to return. Changing pages does not interrupt sensors or transmission.

Turn rate is shown in degrees per minute, not bank angle. The centre tick is zero; the three marks on each side are one-third, two-thirds and the selected maximum. Tap the maximum-scale number below either needle to open Turn scale. Choose 60-720 degrees/minute in steps of 30 using the wheel or arrows. The default and reset value is 540, with ticks at 180, 360 and 540, equivalent to the previous 3, 6 and 9 degrees/second marks. Apply saves the scale for both indicators and future launches; Cancel discards the preview. The number labels the outer tick, not the current rate.

Right deflection means a right turn; left deflection means a left turn. The outer ticks remain at 45 degrees of needle deflection. Beyond them, the needle continues proportionally to a horizontal 90-degree stop at twice the selected rate. With 540 selected, 810 degrees/minute gives 67.5 degrees of deflection and 1080 reaches the stop. The extension has no extra ticks; rates beyond the stop cannot be distinguished. Unavailable data is indicated separately.

Both turn indicators use a two-stage low-pass filter with a 0.25-second time constant per stage, reaching roughly 90% of a sustained change after one second without overshoot. This reduces jitter but adds lag to the turn indication only, not pitch/roll estimation, raw recordings or the XCSoar attitude feed. It is distinct from experimental gyro low-pass filtering.

Turn rate uses calibrated gyro rates and the untrimmed attitude estimate, not changes in magnetic heading. The damped slip ball represents specific force, not aerodynamic sideslip angle. Neither instrument is independent of the phone's sensor limitations.

Compass on the turn-and-slip page

The dedicated page includes the turn instrument, slip ball and a round compass, without the horizon, altitude/speed tapes or correction-gain readout. In portrait, the compass sits above the dial; in landscape, the dial is on the left and the compass on the right. Both round instruments have matching diameters and shrink as needed to fit, while leaving the menu accessible.

The compass shows magnetic heading, not relative turn or rotation speed. Read the curved card against its fixed centre line or use the numeric heading below. N/E/S/W mark cardinal directions; other card numbers are tens of degrees, so 12 means 120 degrees. Larger nearby headings appear to the left of the centre line, like a mechanical compass.

The compass omits the changing mode label below its face. In Diagnostics, MAGNETIC means active magnetic correction and GYRO_HOLD means propagation from an established magnetic reference. Without a reference, the compass card is empty and its numeric heading shows dashes; relative turn is never substituted.

Magnetometer quality checks may reject a nearby magnet as interference. The compass is not a separate heading sensor, true-heading calculation or independent backup; its styling does not guarantee accuracy. The main horizon's heading bar retains its own labels.

Held displays and degraded status

When attitude becomes unusable, the previous usable sensor frame may remain visible for up to five seconds, after which unavailable readings are cleared. The dedicated turn-and-slip page also holds briefly when turn-rate calculation becomes unavailable. ATTITUDE HELD or DISPLAY HELD shows the original measurement's increasing age: retained slip, compass and other indications are old data, not new readings. GYRO_HOLD in Diagnostics describes heading propagation, not this display-cache hold.

Usable data resumes immediately when it returns. Continuous faults do not restart the hold timer, and no frame is invented if none was previously usable. Stopping, changing orientation or calibration, or starting a new session clears the cache. Diagnostics and recordings report actual sensor faults immediately; the XCSoar transmitter never treats the display cache as fresh attitude. Independent pressure/GPS failures keep their existing freshness checks.

DEGRADED means correction or estimate quality is limited. This label is omitted beneath the dedicated turn-and-slip dial, but remains available in Diagnostics and on the main horizon. Its absence beneath the dial is not evidence of a healthy estimate. Unavailable, held, test and recording indications can still appear there.

Trim, labels and correction gain

Pitch display trim spans -30 to +30 degrees in 0.5-degree steps. Positive trim adds nose-up pitch, including in XCSoar output. It does not replace calibration; recorded raw attitude remains untrimmed. Angle number size changes bank/pitch scale labels from 10 to 24, default 14.

Correction gain ranges from 0.00 to 1.00 in 0.01 steps, default 0.10. Lower values reduce accelerometer influence but increase reliance on the gyro and drift risk. Higher values increase susceptibility to acceleration being interpreted as tilt. Zero disables correction after startup, not startup alignment. Changes apply live and are saved; no setting guarantees accurate aircraft attitude.

Gyro low-pass (experimental)

Open Gyro low-pass from the menu. It defaults to Off; enabling it selects 10 Hz. Use the slider or arrows to choose 5-30 Hz in 1 Hz steps. Changes apply live and persist for the next launch; closing the panel does not cancel them. Applied shows the service setting, and GYRO LPF with the frequency appears on both instrument views while enabled. Switch it off to restore bypass.

This filters gyro data before attitude estimation, affecting the horizon and attitude sent to XCSoar, not just needle movement. Lower cutoffs suppress more high-frequency motion but add lag. The accelerometer remains unfiltered, so the timing difference needs evaluation. Filtering cannot repair vibration aliasing or sensor clipping. A cutoff at or above half the delivered sample rate makes attitude unavailable rather than silently bypassing the filter.

Use only for controlled ground/replay experiments; no cutoff is validated for flight. Compare raw sensor recordings, drift and response to actual rotation, not merely smoothness. Raw sensor and calibration data remain unfiltered; recordings include the cutoff and timestamped changes. User-facing replay is not currently implemented.

Altitude, QNH and flight level

Enable Altitude tape and choose Barometric or GPS, in metres or feet. There is no silent source fallback. For barometric altitude, tap its value or choose Adjust altitude / QNH, use a known field elevation or set QNH, and select Apply. Cancel discards the preview. Supported QNH is 850-1100 hPa; the standard reset selects 1013.25 hPa, not local QNH.

GPS MSL and GPS WGS84 identify different altitude datums. QNH cannot adjust GPS altitude. The optional Flight level box always needs fresh barometer pressure at 1013.25 hPa, independent of QNH or the tape's source. FL 035 means 3,500 feet of pressure altitude, not ground clearance. Missing data shows dashes.

Ground speed and grid

GPS ground speed supports km/h, kt and mph. Optional approach, Va and Vne references must satisfy 0 < approach < Va < Vne; no default limits are supplied. These are markings on ground speed, not airspeed, and must not be used to decide stall or overspeed safety. The optional grid is a reference plane, not terrain or synthetic vision.

Background operation

Opening the app starts a foreground service that can process sensors while XCSoar is visible. Switching apps does not stop it. Select Quit or Stop horizon in the Android notification to stop processing and transmission.

Android 13+ requests notification permission. Precise location is needed for GPS displays, and Nearby devices permission for Bluetooth on newer Android versions. No USB debugging, developer mode or overlay permission is needed. The app does not request background-location permission.

GPS requests can continue behind another app while GPS displays are enabled. Disable both GPS ground speed and GPS altitude, or stop the service, to stop those requests. Manufacturer battery settings may interrupt background processing; unrestricted operation can increase battery consumption and is not a guarantee. The service does not start at boot, and an interrupted recording does not automatically resume.

Recording and diagnostics

  1. Open Diagnostics and select Start recording.
  2. Perform a controlled ground test, noting the mounting, reference pose, motion and settings.
  3. Select Stop recording, then open Recordings to export with Android's document picker or delete a capture.

Recordings contain sensor, attitude, calibration and diagnostic data, including mounting metadata, correction-gain changes and gyro low-pass cutoffs and timestamped changes. Raw sensor and calibration data remain unfiltered; raw recorded pitch is untrimmed. Current recordings use schema 8; the decoder accepts schemas 1-7 with the gyro filter Off. Optional GPS/pressure tape values and flight level are not currently recorded. User-facing replay is not implemented. Files remain app-private until explicitly exported; review metadata before sharing.

Storage limits and interruptions can leave incomplete data. Check recording status rather than assuming a file is complete. Uninstalling or clearing app storage erases private recordings, settings and calibration. Export important recordings first; this does not back up all settings.

Troubleshooting

XCSoar receives nothing

Check accepted calibration, fresh attitude, enabled transmission and the GDL90 driver. Match UDP ports and confirm the receiver's address, or check Bluetooth pairing and that XCSoar is the client. Use the connection mode's offline Help page.

The horizon moves during acceleration

Accelerometers cannot distinguish gravity from sustained acceleration. Changing gain trades acceleration-induced error against gyro drift; it does not remove the limitation. For a wrong stationary pose, check mounting, orientation, calibration, known ground pitch and trim.

The turn-and-slip page or needle is unavailable

Enable Turn and slip page, not just Turn indicator, and close menus before swiping. The needle needs usable attitude and gyro rates; missing rates, stale/held/invalid attitude or near-vertical pitch makes it unavailable after the bounded display hold. A retained needle is not a current turn-rate reading. The slip ball has separate validity checks.

Wing-tilt calibration reports cross-axis motion

Review the measured fit and moving-sample counts. Avoid twisting, lifting or moving supports between captures; use one fixed fore-aft pivot. At least 95% of measured rotation energy must align with that axis. Flat mounting alone is not a reason to reject wing-tilt calibration. See calibration guidance.

Attitude becomes unavailable with gyro low-pass enabled

Inspect the exact reason in Diagnostics. The cutoff must be below half the delivered sample rate. Return the filter to Off for comparison during controlled ground tests; do not hide sensor faults by changing settings. See gyro low-pass limitations.

The compass is empty, takes time to align or rarely corrects magnetically

An empty card and numeric dashes mean no magnetic reference is available; relative turn is not a substitute. Inspect Heading reference, Magnetic field, Magnetic sample heading and Last magnetic correction in Diagnostics. Initial alignment needs fresh usable attitude, a horizontal turn reference, RUNNING estimator status, suitable sensor accuracy, 20-70 uT total field, at least 5 uT horizontal field and rotation at most 20 degrees/second. Later corrections require two seconds of agreement; repeatedly crossing the field limit restarts that check. Try a stationary position away from magnetic cases, holders, chargers and metal. Another compass showing a value does not establish accuracy. See compass behavior.

The turn needle moves beyond the outer tick

The needle continues through an unmarked extension to a horizontal stop at twice the selected outer-tick rate. Rates beyond the stop cannot be distinguished. Tap the maximum-scale number to adjust Turn scale; it labels the outer tick, not the current rate.

The display says ATTITUDE HELD, DISPLAY HELD or STALE DATA

A retained display is old data, not current motion. Brief dropouts have a five-second hold limit, with the original measurement's increasing age shown. Continuous faults do not restart the timer. Stale or unavailable indications must not be read as live measurements, and the transmitter never uses the held display as fresh attitude. See held-display behavior.

DEGRADED is missing beneath the turn-and-slip dial

The dedicated dial omits this label. Check Diagnostics or the main horizon for degraded status; the absence of the label beneath the dial does not establish estimate quality or accuracy.

Altitude, flight level or speed shows dashes

Check source availability and freshness, GPS reception and precise-location permission. Flight level always needs a barometer, even when the altitude tape uses GPS. QNH and differing altitude datums can explain different displayed values.

Processing stops or the notification is missing

Check Android Settings > Apps > GlideHorizon > Notifications and the phone's battery/background restrictions. A hidden notification does not necessarily mean the service stopped. Test the intended device and power arrangement on the ground.

An APK update is rejected

Check the release instructions and signing compatibility. Do not uninstall as a first step: doing so erases private recordings, settings and calibration. Export recordings you need before removing the app.

A setting returns after reopening

Correction gain and Gyro low-pass changes are saved immediately; closing their panels is not an undo operation. QNH and Turn scale use explicit Apply/Cancel, and cancelling discards their previews.
